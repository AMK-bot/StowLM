## What must be true

**Full-ship, multi-port planning works; these rates hold worldwide; the extra work is avoidable; operational use needs terms that permit it: today's Muse Glimmer usage policy prohibits use related to operating transportation technologies where harm is possible; a deployment would need Meta's permission or a base model whose terms allow it, plus class approval; none of it has been measured.** Earlier departure must free usable vessel time. This is a gross opportunity scenario, not an audited global loss, net savings, software revenue or a result achieved. Lift prices proxy what carriers are charged; billed reductions can transfer revenue between carrier and terminal and are not world marginal-cost savings; carrier planner labor must sit outside terminal tariff labor, and charter time excludes fuel dollars. World throughput is treated as productive load/discharge handling, without another transshipment multiplier; audit restows already included before adding extra lifts. Box mix, one container per lift, fleet-weighted time, rates, wages and adoption need validation. The planner pool includes productive work, so capture—not the full pool—is the potential benefit. Costs of compute, integration and support must be deducted in a business case; the engine is not competitive at large scale yet. Today's eligible feeder-call count is unknown, no operational saving is measured, and the pilot, legacy throughput-adoption scenarios and global-prize tables must not be added. The 'runs the loop' sentence cites task 3.1 (final-3) with its D284 label.
