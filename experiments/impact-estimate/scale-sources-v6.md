# Scale inputs for the v6 impact estimate

Research date: 2026-11-01. Public sources only. These are context and projection inputs, not StowLM results. No global observed restow/rehandle rate was verified; any such rate in the calculator must remain an explicit assumption.

## World volume and denominator

- [UNCTAD Data Hub, maritime and other transport](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/insights/theme/245), data updated **2026-04-15**: **920 million TEU** handled in ports worldwide in **2024**. **Directly verified:** although the browser retrieval returned HTTP 403, a direct shell HTTPS request returned HTTP 200 and the complete HTML, including that statement and update date. The HTML links to the [official chart CSV](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/sites/unctadstat/files/unctadstat-data-files/t11d33dm02_fig_global_20260415.csv), also fetched directly: its **2024** row is **920.300805**, with units **millions of TEU** established by the page. Thus **920,300,805 TEU/year** is a direct series value, not a search-summary inference. Rounded **920 million TEU/year** is sufficient for prose.
- [World Bank metadata, UNCTAD-sourced container port traffic](https://databank.worldbank.org/metadataglossary/world-development-indicators/series/IS.SHP.GOOD.TU), reference period through **2024**, accessed research date: throughput includes coastal/international traffic and empty units. Intermediate transshipment counts once off-loaded and once loaded again. TEU is size-normalized throughput, not a count of physical boxes, calls, ships, unique cargo, or vessel capacity.
- Conversion from TEU throughput to physical productive port lifts requires a stated **TEU per physical container** assumption or a measured mix. Keep local and global calculations distinct. Do not add origin/destination and intermediate transshipment again: the throughput already includes those handling events. Yard rehandles and restows must be defined as extra to the productive lifts, with a clear denominator.

## Restows and yard rehandles: evidence and limits

- [Jensen and Ajspur, *Revenue management in liner shipping: Addressing the vessel capacity challenge*, Maritime Transport Research **3 (2022), 100069**, §7.2.1 / Table 10](https://www.sealytix.com/media/gm4flxf5/revenuemanagementinlinershippingaddressingthevesselcapacitychallenge.pdf), [university bibliographic record](https://researcher.itu.dk/da/publications/revenue-management-in-liner-shipping-addressing-the-vessel-capaci/): **context only, never a rate calibration or avoidable-work estimate.** Its deliberately constructed stress voyage has productive baseline **8,977 crane moves**. With design, hydrostatics, GM and overstowage, the repositioning variants require **12,531.0 / 10,810.7** moves, including **3,554.0 / 1,833.7** additional moves: **28.4% / 17.0%** of their total moves, rounded. These are the model's **minimized required moves under its fixed cargo/load order**, not waste removable by a better stowage plan. Neither endpoint nor their midpoint can source a worldwide restow assumption. Removing such required moves would require changing the underlying cargo/load order or feasible operational choices.
- [Zhao and Goodchild, *The impact of truck arrival information on container terminal rehandling*, Transportation Research E **46 (2010), 327–343**, DOI **10.1016/j.tre.2009.11.007**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1366554509001409): distinguishes productive crane moves from unproductive yard relocations. Its extreme random retrieval example has **78 unproductive** and **48 productive** moves, giving **48/(78+48) = 38.10%** productive efficiency (computed). The authors expressly say this is a lower efficiency bound, **not expected terminal operations**. Do not promote this to a market rate.
- Secondary-only lead, unsuitable as the principal model input until the underlying paper is retrieved: [Mudzamba's **2010** Massey thesis, printed p.30](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/bitstreams/1b75d683-06dc-4daf-92d5-e660f12c5dd4/download) describes Chen, Lin and Juang's **2000** study at Yang Ming, Kaohsiung. It reports yard shift counts equal to **9%** / **17%** of containers loaded for straddle / RTG blocks, and housekeeping equal to **21%** of discharged containers. These denominators differ from total crane moves; they are historical site-specific yard figures, not vessel restow shares. [Original DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/030888300286608) was HTTP 403 during research.

Recommendation: use explicit low/mid/high assumptions for **extra moves per productive physical lift**, separately from the fraction of that work avoided. State that local logs must replace them. A percentage of total moves `s` converts to extra/productive ratio `s/(1-s)`; it must not be inserted unchanged. Do not stack a yard estimate and a restow estimate without checking their scopes for duplication.

Scope: container TOS covering vessel/yard operations and scheduling, on-premises/cloud and terminal automation types. These are **commercial analyst estimates**, not audited operator spending or obtainable StowLM revenue. The public methodology is insufficient to independently reproduce the figures. The page says **5.85% CAGR**; use the base-year market amount rather than importing its growth path.
- [Dataintelo, digital stowage planning software market](https://dataintelo.com/report/digital-stowage-planning-software-market), updated **April 2026**, says data verified through that month: publisher estimate **USD 1.8 billion in 2025**, projected **USD 4.1 billion in 2034**. The two market definitions overlap. **Never add them**, add their forecast growth to operational savings, or treat any market percentage as adoption evidence. Market estimates establish that customers already buy planning/terminal systems; a StowLM product would need operator validation, integration, support and willingness to pay.

## Crane energy and emissions

- [PEMA, *Energy and Environmental Efficiency in Ports & Terminals*, first published **June 2011**, §3.2 printed p.15](https://pema.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/PEMA-IP2-Energy-and-Environmental-Efficiency-in-Ports-and-Terminals.pdf): measurements on relatively large modern electric ship-to-shore cranes give **6 kWh per move**, including **2 kWh auxiliary energy per move** at **30 moves/hour**. Average energy is not marginal avoided energy. If auxiliaries stay on, a defensible **assumption** is to credit only **6−2 = 4 kWh** per avoided electric quay move. Different crane heights, regeneration, box weights, spreader operations and duty cycles can change it. Do not apply this automatically to yard/hatch lifts.
- [Official Thailand Greenhouse Gas Management Organization, CFP emission-factor table, electricity row **43**](https://thaicarbonlabel.tgo.or.th/index.php?lang=EN&mod=Y0hKdlpIVmpkSE5mWlcxcGMzTnBiMjQ9), updated **April 2026**: Thai grid mix, data years **2022–2024**, **0.5562 kgCO2e/kWh**, Thai National LCI Database, LCIA IPCC 2013 GWP 100a. Pairing this with the assumed **4 kWh/move** gives **2.2248 kgCO2e per avoided electric quay move** (computed projection). This is a Thai grid lifecycle factor; do not apply it to world activity. Net climate benefit needs actual electricity savings, local supply, added computing emissions and lifecycle boundaries.
- **Not directly verified; excluded from the calculator:** indexed [NREL factsheet **NREL/FS-5400-91423, September 2024**, Table 1](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy24osti/91423.pdf) lists **6.5–12 kWh/TEU** vessel-to-dockside energy. Direct download failed during the adopted-review check. Retain only as a research lead until the complete document is opened; its TEU denominator would also need conversion before any comparison with kWh/physical move.

## Conditional berth-emissions method

[US EPA, *Ports Emissions Inventory Guidance*, **EPA-420-B-22-011, April 2022**](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1014J1S.TXT), Appendix E Table E.1: container-ship auxiliary hotelling loads **340 / 600 / 700 / 940 / 970 / 1,000 / 1,200 / 1,320 kW**, for subtype labels **1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 / 5,000 / 8,000 / 12,000 / 14,500 TEU / Largest**. Labels are upper-bound categories: Table 3.4 maps actual **0–999 TEU** to the **1,000 TEU** subtype. Defaults already include load factors; do not multiply again.

Table 3.6 supplies **217 g fuel/kWh** for MGO/MDO auxiliary diesels; §3.5.6 uses **3.206 g CO2/g fuel**. Computed **0.695702 kg CO2/kWh**, or **236.53868 / 417.4212 / 486.9914 / 653.95988 / 674.83094 / 695.702 / 834.8424 / 918.32664 kg CO2/hour** across those loads. Selecting a world-fleet-average load is an **assumption**. These are auxiliary-only combustion CO2 intensities, excluding boilers, methane, nitrous oxide, fuel lifecycle and shore power. Credit only actual critical-path berth hours removed; anchorage and hotelling power differ. Faster crane work does not guarantee earlier departure. Added computing energy also needs accounting.

## World calls, time and vessel opportunity value: broad prize before adoption

The broad prize is conditional **world activity × hypothesized improvement**, before any StowLM market-capture fraction. It is not achieved saving or software revenue. Keep prize and adoption scenarios separate.

- [UNCTAD *Review of Maritime Transport 2025*, Chapter IV, §A.1, printed p.99 and Figure IV.1 p.100](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/rmt2025ch4_en.pdf), published **2025-09-24**: **directly downloaded and read** despite browser HTTP 403; shell HTTPS request returned HTTP 200, **2,527,246 bytes**, a **36-page** chapter PDF. Complete printed pp.99–100 confirm container calls at **about 260,000** in the second semester of **2023**, with a similar level in both semesters of **2024**. Thus **about 520,000 calls/year = 2 × about 260,000** remains a **rounded derived activity proxy**, not an exact annual total. Figure IV.1's note restricts ships to **1,000 GT and above**, with IMO numbers, AIS entry/exit and low-speed filters, and consecutive same-port visits combined under its rules. Do not mix with all-vessel totals. Figure IV.6 port time is a **median**, not an aggregate-time mean.
- [World Bank/S&P, *CPPI 2020–2024*, **2025**, §5.3 printed p.48](https://ppp.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/The%20Container%20Port%20Performance%20Index%202020%20to%202024%20-%20Trends%20and%20Lessons%20Learned.pdf): **175,152** recorded calls at **403** ports in **2024** is a **sample**, not all world container calls. Table 5.1 p.44 gives **21.8 / 29.4 / 35.0 / 39.4 / 43.8 / 47.9 / 53.5 / 63.2 / 86.7 hours** for call sizes **<500 / 501–1,000 / 1,001–1,500 / 1,501–2,000 / 2,001–2,500 / 2,501–3,000 / 3,001–4,000 / 4,001–6,000 / >6,000 physical moves**. These are **unweighted means across the top 25 economies in each call-size column**, not a world-fleet mean. Figure 3.6 p.24 says average berth share **75%**; applying it uniformly is an assumption. CPPI port time runs from anchorage/pilot arrival to berth departure, excluding outbound port transit. Assumed representative port-time values and a reducible fraction may support projections, but a sourced fleet-average time was not verified here.
- [VHBS New ConTex, table dated **2026-10-29**](https://www.vhbs.de/index.php?id=28&L=1): assessed **12-month time-charter USD/day** values **17,855** for **1,100 TEU**, **35,982** for **1,800 TEU**, **39,725** for **2,500 TEU**, **46,570** for **3,500 TEU**, **59,985** for **4,250 TEU**, **66,239** for **5,700 TEU**, **72,722** for **6,500 TEU**. Dots in the German-formatted source table are thousands separators. These are sampled ship/contract assessments, **not a world fleet weighted average** or full operating cost. Selecting low/mid/high world average proxies from this range is an **assumption**. Hire continues when time is saved; the result values potentially redeployable vessel time, not automatically reduced cash hire. It excludes bunkers, port dues and delay penalties. A valid model needs demand/schedule conditions permitting use of the freed capacity.

Port-time projection rule: use `calls × assumed representative port hours × assumed physically reducible fraction × assumed hire USD/day / hours per day`. For berth-specific changes, apply berth share before the reducible fraction. Do not separately monetize crane-time savings already contained in the same reduced vessel time, or count anchorage time as berth time. Fleet/calendar scheduling and critical paths are transfer gates.

## Planner attention and sourced wage context

- [Yasuoka and Jensen, *AI, Making Software for Humans*, JSAI **2019**, printed p.2](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/pjsai/JSAI2019/0/JSAI2019_2C5E502/_pdf/-char/ja), §3.4: skilled stowage planners usually spend **2–3 hours** allocating all containers for each port. It does not identify a representative ship-size distribution, a present global sample, intake time, repeated-change time or a measured automation saving. Applying this per world call, adding carrier and terminal planners, or multiplying by an improvement fraction are **assumptions**. Do not count the same attention twice when adding intake and rework savings.
- [U.S. BLS, Occupational Outlook Handbook: Logisticians](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Business-and-financial/logisticians.htm), modified **2026-08-27**, **May 2025** wage data: median **USD 82,320/year**, lower tenth below **USD 50,890**, upper tenth above **USD 133,160**. At an **assumed 2,080 paid hours/year**, computed median **USD 39.576923/hour** (round **USD 39.58**). These are U.S. occupation-wide cash wages; not Thai/global wages, specific terminal-planner pay, benefits or loaded labor cost. A world wage proxy or benefit multiplier stays an assumption. Labor value is redeployable attention, not automatic payroll reduction; exclude labor already embedded in any other charge-equivalent total.

[IMO announcement, **2021-03-23**, Fourth IMO GHG Study **2020**](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/pages/whatsnew-1596) reports **1,056 million tonnes of CO2** from **total shipping in 2018**, **2.89%** of global anthropogenic CO2 that year. ## Global electricity factor

[IEA, *Electricity 2025*, Emissions chapter](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2025/emissions), **2025** report, [report PDF, p.67](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/0f028d5f-26b1-47ca-ad2a-5ca3103d070a/Electricity2025.pdf): **445 g CO2/kWh in 2024**, computed as world electricity-generation CO2 divided by total generation. Converted **0.445 kg CO2/kWh**; paired with assumed marginal **4 kWh per electric quay lift**, computed **1.78 kg CO2/lift**. Applying the generation average to global quay activity is an **assumption**, not a port-weighted electricity factor or marginal avoided-generation estimate. It excludes fuel lifecycle and non-CO2 gases, so label this **CO2**, not CO2e. Exclude diesel yard/hatch moves unless their separate activity, fuel and energy model is supplied. Do not use the Thai lifecycle factor for this world projection or compare the different system boundaries as if identical.

Distinguish **total cost/emissions exposure**, **potential physically avoidable amount**, and **potential StowLM capture** in names and tables. Total vessel port-time exposure is `calls × port hours × daily hire / 24`; it includes productive work and required waiting and is **not all waste**. Potential berth benefit is only `exposure × berth share × reducible fraction`. Extra quay-lift exposure may be costed as an inefficiency pool only after an assumed extra-lift ratio and productive/quay coverage are declared; it is not all avoidable. Avoided planner time needs its own explicit improvement fraction. A large total exposure must never be relabelled as the saving obtainable by this product. Charge equivalents, wage opportunities and vessel time can overlap economically; report components and explicit boundaries before presenting any combined total.

The adopted **5% / 10% / 15%** restow sensitivity supersedes any use of the constructed **17.0% / 28.4%** cases as world-rate endpoints. It remains an assumption even where a numerical value happens to resemble a row in that paper. Published tariffs value what a carrier might be charged; avoided resource cost, loss of terminal revenue and carrier benefit are different quantities. Broader vessel and multi-port planning must work before this empty-arrival pilot could reach the ship-restow component. Existing throughput may include restows under some reporting conventions: treating it as entirely productive handling requires an explicit assumption and audit before adding the extra-lift pool.

## What must become true to scale

The path is more compute for the search, a larger model for reading; neither is a proven scaling law. First prove intake/reader accuracy and planner attention saved within the current envelope using the frozen pipeline. Next validate occupied arrival states, future loading, larger box counts, broader rules and checker coverage on larger feeders. Finally require operator-grade data exchange, yard changes, crane scheduling and carrier approval in a supervised external pilot before projecting wider adoption. No present small-map percentage supplies any of these transfer factors.
