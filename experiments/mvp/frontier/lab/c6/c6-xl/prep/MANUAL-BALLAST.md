Neither script is imported by the benchmark solver or used as solver ballast, cargo, placement or warm-start data. They preserve the exact original C1/C2/C3 fleet-fixture cargo, hull, gear, limits, tank geometry/capacities and route. Any result is a **new synthetic declared ballast recipe**, not an original manual pass. Earlier full-check and bounded original-finisher failures remain unchanged.

`manual_ballast.py` reuses frozen `solver/ballast_opt.py` and `solver/physics.py`. A fail-closed source transformation removes only the optimizer's preferred-four-group restriction and its search-only 98% fill-regime bounds. All existing adjustable P/S groups plus the inherited HEEL split are exposed; physical tank fractions remain [0,1]. Native physics evaluates every derivative/trial, existing discontinuity detection rebuilds derivatives, and a fresh independent full voyage checker decides acceptance. The C1 pilot is preregistered at 300s search and 600s whole-worker limit. Its first full three-phase oracle took 75.3s, which can starve a ten-direction Jacobian; no claim of feasibility or infeasibility follows from exhaustion.

`manual_hydro.py` is a separate, preregistered cheap preconditioner. It considers at most three targets in order: original departure displacement at zero trim; upright design draught minus0.5m; upright design draught minus1.0m. Linear programs operate on existing tank fraction directions and actual native sounding/loading moments; direct native floating equilibrium with actual liquid CG checks the hydrostatic corrections. It never computes a GZ curve for a Jacobian. Each target produces at most one candidate for the unchanged full three-phase voyage checker. Search300s, whole-worker600s,56GB, one numerical thread and nice5 requested. A local linear target failure is not physical infeasibility. This alternative must wait for an explicit CPU handoff and separate launch authorization.
